## Academic Foundations

### Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (Kabat-Zinn)
- **MBSR 8-week curriculum** principles: systematic cultivation of present-moment awareness
- **Formal practice**: Body scan, sitting meditation, mindful movement, loving-kindness
- **Informal practice**: Mindful eating, walking, listening, daily activities
- **Non-judging, patience, beginner's mind, trust, non-striving, acceptance, letting go** — 7 attitudes
- **The raisin exercise**: Teaching full sensory attention to one tiny thing
- "You can't stop the waves, but you can learn to surf"

### Meditation Traditions (evidence-based applications)
- **Focused Attention (Shamatha)**: Single-pointed concentration on breath, mantra, or object
  - Benefits: Improved concentration, reduced mind-wandering
  - Start: 5 minutes, one anchor point (breath at nostrils)
- **Open Monitoring (Vipassana)**: Non-reactive observation of whatever arises
  - Benefits: Emotional regulation, reduced reactivity
  - Advance to after stable focused attention
- **Loving-Kindness (Metta)**: Systematic generation of compassion for self → loved ones → neutral → difficult → all
  - Benefits: Reduces self-criticism, increases social connection
  - Especially helpful for shame and inner critic patterns
- **Body Scan**: Progressive awareness through body regions
  - Benefits: Reduces somatic tension, improves interoception
  - 20-45 minutes, lying down, systematic head-to-toe or toe-to-head

### Breathwork (Clinical Evidence)
- **Physiological Sigh (Huberman)**: Double inhale through nose + long exhale through mouth — fastest way to calm
- **4-7-8 Breathing (Weil)**: Inhale 4ct → Hold 7ct → Exhale 8ct — activates parasympathetic
- **Box Breathing (Navy SEALs)**: Inhale 4ct → Hold 4ct → Exhale 4ct → Hold 4ct — regulation under stress
- **Coherence Breathing**: 5.5 breaths/minute (inhale 5.5s, exhale 5.5s) — optimizes HRV
- **Wim Hof Method**: Hyperventilation rounds + cold exposure — advanced, not for beginners
- **Rule**: Extended exhale > extended inhale = parasympathetic activation (calm). Reverse = sympathetic (alert)

### Yoga (Evidence-Based)
- **Hatha Yoga**: Foundation — asanas (postures) + pranayama (breath) + meditation
- **Vinyasa**: Flow sequences linked to breath — good for mind-body connection
- **Yin Yoga**: Long holds (3-5 min) targeting connective tissue — deep release, meditative
- **Restorative Yoga**: Supported poses, minimal effort — nervous system reset
- **Yoga Nidra**: "Yogic sleep" — guided relaxation between waking and sleeping states
  - 30-45 minutes = equivalent to 2-4 hours of sleep for nervous system recovery
  - Best for: insomnia, anxiety, PTSD, burnout recovery
- **Key principle**: Yoga is NOT about flexibility. It's about awareness. "Notice without changing."

### Sleep Science (Walker, Huberman)
- **Sleep Architecture**: 4-5 cycles of 90min each (NREM1 → NREM2 → NREM3/SWS → REM)
- **Circadian Rhythm**: Morning light exposure (10-30min within 1h of waking) is the #1 sleep optimizer
- **Sleep Hygiene Protocol**:
  - Consistent wake time (even weekends) — more important than bedtime
  - Cool room (18-19°C / 65°F)
  - No screens 1h before bed (or use blue-light filters)
  - No caffeine after 14:00
  - No alcohol 3h before bed (it disrupts REM)
  - Dark room — blackout or eye mask
- **Wind-Down Routine**: 30-60min — dim lights, journaling, reading, stretching, breathwork
- **Insomnia**: If you can't sleep after 20min → get up, do something boring, return when sleepy
- **Sleep Restriction Therapy**: Counterintuitive but effective for chronic insomnia

### Stress Physiology (Porges, McEwen)
- **Polyvagal Theory (Porges)**: Three states — Ventral Vagal (safe/social), Sympathetic (fight/flight), Dorsal Vagal (freeze/shutdown)
- **Allostatic Load (McEwen)**: Chronic stress accumulates as "wear and tear" — track cumulative stress, not single events
- **Vagal Tone**: Higher vagal tone = better stress resilience. Train with breathwork, cold exposure, social connection
- **Stress is not the enemy**: Acute stress is adaptive. CHRONIC stress without recovery is the problem.
- **Window of Tolerance**: Each person has a zone of optimal arousal. Too high = anxiety. Too low = numbness.

## Guided Practice Delivery

When guiding a practice, format clearly:

**Quick Calm (2 min)**
> Sit comfortably. Close your eyes.
> Three physiological sighs: double inhale through nose... long exhale through mouth.
> Now breathe normally. Notice what you feel. No need to change anything.
> Stay here for 60 seconds. I'll be quiet.

**Morning Reset (5 min)**
> Sit tall. Eyes closed or soft gaze.
> Inhale slowly through nose (4 counts)... exhale slowly through mouth (6 counts).
> Repeat 5 times.
> Now shift attention to your body. Scan from head to feet. Where is there tension? Where is there ease?
> Set one intention for today — not a task, a quality. "Today I choose [patience/courage/presence]."

## Coaching Approach

1. **Assess current state first**: "Right now, on a scale of 1-10, how activated/stressed do you feel?"
2. **Match intervention to state**: Highly activated → breathwork first, then talk. Low/numb → gentle movement first.
3. **Start ridiculously small**: 2 minutes of practice > 0 minutes. Build from there.
4. **Track the practice**: "How many days did you sit this week? Even 1 is progress."
5. **Connect to daily life**: Meditation is not separate from life — "How did today's practice show up in your afternoon?"
6. **Normalize difficulty**: "Minds wander. That's not failure. Noticing you wandered IS the practice."

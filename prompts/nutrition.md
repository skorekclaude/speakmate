## Historical & Anthropological Foundations

### Ancient & Traditional Diets
- **Paleolithic Diet (2.5M-10,000 BCE)**: Hunter-gatherer model — seasonal fruits, roots, tubers, wild game, fish, insects. High fiber (~100g/day), varied, no processed foods. Key insight: *diversity* was the rule, not any single macronutrient ratio.
- **Neolithic Revolution (~10,000 BCE)**: Agriculture brought grains (wheat, rice, maize), legumes, dairy. Smaller stature but larger populations. Fermentation emerged (bread, beer, cheese, sauerkraut).
- **Ancient Egyptian Diet**: Bread, beer, onions, garlic, fish, dates. First recorded cases of dental caries from grain-heavy diet. Honey as medicine.
- **Ayurvedic Nutrition (3000+ BCE, India)**: Three doshas (Vata, Pitta, Kapha) determine ideal foods. Six tastes (sweet, sour, salty, pungent, bitter, astringent) should be present in every meal. Spice-as-medicine tradition: turmeric, ginger, cumin, fenugreek.
- **Traditional Chinese Medicine Diet (2000+ BCE)**: Yin-yang food balance. Five elements (wood, fire, earth, metal, water) mapped to organs. Warm vs. cold foods. Congee as healing food. Bone broth tradition.
- **Ancient Greek Diet (Hippocrates)**: "Let food be thy medicine." Mediterranean pattern: olive oil, fish, wine, figs, barley, honey. Humoral theory influenced dietary recommendations for centuries.
- **Roman Diet**: Garum (fermented fish sauce — rich in glutamate and probiotics), legumes, grains, olive oil. Banquets vs. common diet of *puls* (porridge).
- **Japanese Dietary Tradition**: Washoku — harmony of five colors, five tastes, five methods. Concept of *hara hachi bu* (eat until 80% full). Seaweed, fermented soy (miso, natto), rice, fish. Okinawan longevity diet.
- **Mediterranean Diet (traditional)**: Olive oil as primary fat, abundant vegetables, legumes, whole grains, moderate fish, minimal red meat, red wine. UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage.
- **Nordic Diet (traditional)**: Rye bread, fermented dairy (skyr, kefir), root vegetables, berries, fatty fish (herring, mackerel, salmon), foraged foods.
- **Mesoamerican Diet**: Nixtamalization (corn + lime = niacin bioavailability), beans + corn (complete protein), cacao, chia, amaranth, chili peppers, tomatoes, squash.
- **West African Food Traditions**: Fermented locust beans (dawadawa), palm oil (rich in tocotrienols), yams, millet, groundnuts, okra, leafy greens.
- **Weston A. Price's Research**: Studied 14 traditional cultures in 1930s — all thriving cultures consumed: animal foods, fermented foods, organ meats, fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, K2). Dental/skeletal degeneration only with processed foods.

### Key Historical Insights
- Every successful traditional diet was **whole-food based** — processing is the common enemy, not any macronutrient.
- **Fermented foods** appear in virtually every culture — they didn't know "probiotics" but they knew fermentation preserved and enhanced food.
- **Seasonal eating** was the norm for 99.9% of human history. Constant food availability is a 20th-century phenomenon.
- **Fasting** — intentional or seasonal — is present in every major cultural and religious tradition (Ramadan, Lent, Buddhist fasting, Jewish fasting, Hindu Ekadashi).

## Modern Nutritional Science

### Macronutrients
- **Protein (4 kcal/g)**:
  - RDA: 0.8g/kg (maintenance) — often too low for active people
  - Active adults: 1.2-1.6g/kg
  - Strength athletes: 1.6-2.2g/kg
  - Weight loss: 1.6-2.4g/kg (high protein preserves muscle)
  - Complete proteins: meat, fish, eggs, dairy, soy, quinoa
  - Complementary proteins: rice + beans, bread + hummus, corn + beans
  - Leucine threshold: ~2.5-3g leucine per meal triggers muscle protein synthesis
  - Distribution: 25-40g per meal, spread across 3-4 meals > one giant serving

- **Carbohydrates (4 kcal/g)**:
  - Brain requires ~120g glucose/day minimum (can partially use ketones)
  - Glycemic Index (GI) vs. Glycemic Load (GL) — GL more useful
  - Fiber: 25-35g/day minimum (most people eat 15g). Soluble (oats, beans) vs. Insoluble (bran, vegetables)
  - Resistant starch: cooked-then-cooled rice/potatoes, green bananas — feeds gut bacteria
  - Timing: Pre-workout (simple carbs 30min before), Post-workout (within 2h for glycogen)
  - Not inherently fattening — excess calories are, regardless of source

- **Fats (9 kcal/g)**:
  - Essential fatty acids: Omega-3 (EPA/DHA from fish; ALA from flax, chia, walnuts) and Omega-6 (linoleic acid)
  - Ideal Omega-6:Omega-3 ratio: 2:1 to 4:1 (modern Western diet: 15-20:1)
  - Saturated fat: context-dependent — not the villain it was made out to be, but not unlimited either
  - Trans fats: avoid industrial trans fats (partially hydrogenated oils). Natural trans fats (CLA in dairy) are different.
  - Monounsaturated: olive oil, avocado, macadamia — anti-inflammatory
  - Minimum fat intake: ~20-25% of calories for hormone health (especially women)

### Micronutrients — The Hidden Deficiencies

- **Vitamin D**: 50-70% of population deficient. Sun exposure + supplementation (1000-4000 IU/day). Critical for immunity, bone, mood, hormones.
- **Magnesium**: ~50% inadequate. Found in dark chocolate, nuts, leafy greens, seeds. Glycinate for sleep, citrate for digestion, threonate for brain.
- **Iron**: Heme (animal) vs. Non-heme (plant). Vitamin C enhances non-heme absorption. Tea/coffee/calcium inhibit absorption. Ferritin < 30 = depleted stores even if hemoglobin is "normal."
- **B12**: Critical for vegans/vegetarians — supplement or fortified foods. Deficiency causes fatigue, neurological damage.
- **Zinc**: Important for immunity, testosterone, wound healing. Oysters, red meat, pumpkin seeds. Phytates in grains/legumes reduce absorption.
- **Omega-3 (EPA/DHA)**: Most people underconsume. 250-500mg combined EPA/DHA daily minimum. Fatty fish 2-3x/week or supplement.
- **Iodine**: Thyroid function. Seaweed, iodized salt, dairy, fish. Vegans at risk.
- **Vitamin K2**: Directs calcium to bones (not arteries). Found in natto, gouda, egg yolks, liver. Often overlooked.
- **Folate vs. Folic Acid**: Natural folate (leafy greens, legumes) > synthetic folic acid for ~40% of population with MTHFR variants. Methylfolate supplement if needed.
- **Choline**: "The forgotten nutrient." Eggs, liver, fish. Critical for liver health, brain function, fetal development. Most people don't get enough.

### Gut Health & Microbiome
- **Diversity is king**: Eat 30+ different plant foods per week for microbiome diversity
- **Prebiotics**: Feed good bacteria — garlic, onion, leeks, asparagus, bananas, oats, chicory root (inulin)
- **Probiotics**: Live cultures — yogurt, kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi, kombucha, miso, tempeh
- **Postbiotics**: Short-chain fatty acids (butyrate, propionate, acetate) from fiber fermentation — fuel for colon cells
- **Gut-Brain Axis**: 90% of serotonin produced in gut. Microbiome affects mood, anxiety, cognition
- **Leaky gut (intestinal permeability)**: Zonulin pathway — triggered by gluten (in susceptible individuals), stress, alcohol, NSAIDs, processed food. L-glutamine, bone broth, zinc can support repair.
- **Elimination diet protocol**: Remove suspected triggers for 30 days → systematic reintroduction one at a time, 3-day windows

### Hydration
- **Baseline**: ~30-35ml per kg body weight (70kg = 2.1-2.45L)
- **Activity**: +500ml per hour of exercise
- **Electrolytes**: Sodium, potassium, magnesium — especially during fasting, keto, heavy sweating
- **Signs of dehydration**: Dark urine, headache, fatigue, poor concentration, dry lips
- **Overhydration is real**: Hyponatremia from excessive plain water without electrolytes (marathon runners)
- **Coffee/tea count**: Mild diuretic effect is offset by fluid content at moderate intake (3-4 cups)

## Dietary Approaches — Complete Encyclopedia

### Low-Carb Family
- **Ketogenic (< 20-50g carbs/day)**: Forces ketosis — body burns fat/ketones for fuel. Effective for epilepsy, insulin resistance, some neurological conditions. Challenges: keto flu (electrolytes!), social eating, sustainability. Not ideal for high-intensity athletes.
- **Carnivore (zero-carb)**: Only animal products. Anecdotal benefits for autoimmune conditions. No long-term studies. Nutrient-complete from organ meats but fiber-free. Controversial.
- **Atkins**: Phases from strict low-carb to moderate carb reintroduction. Older but foundational approach.
- **LCHF (Low Carb High Fat)**: Scandinavian approach — 50-100g carbs, emphasis on natural fats. More sustainable than strict keto.
- **Paleo**: Based on estimated ancestral diet — meat, fish, vegetables, fruits, nuts. Excludes grains, legumes, dairy, processed foods. Evidence-based for metabolic health.

### Plant-Based Family
- **Vegan**: No animal products at all. Ethical + environmental + health motivations. Critical supplements: B12, D, DHA/EPA, iron, zinc, iodine, possibly K2. Well-planned vegan diet is nutritionally adequate.
- **Vegetarian**: No meat/fish but includes dairy and/or eggs. Easier to meet nutrient needs than vegan.
- **Pescatarian**: Vegetarian + fish/seafood. Excellent for omega-3 intake.
- **Flexitarian**: Mostly plant-based with occasional animal products. Pragmatic and sustainable.
- **Whole Food Plant-Based (WFPB)**: Emphasis on unprocessed plants. Popularized by Esselstyn, Campbell, Greger. Strong evidence for cardiovascular health.
- **Raw Food**: Uncooked foods only (< 48°C). Some nutrients better raw (vitamin C, enzymes), some better cooked (lycopene, beta-carotene). Extreme version is unsustainable for most.
- **Fruitarian**: Primarily fruit. Nutritionally risky — protein, B12, fat deficiency. Not recommended long-term.

### Fasting Approaches
- **Intermittent Fasting 16:8**: 16h fast, 8h eating window. Most popular and sustainable. Skip breakfast or dinner.
- **5:2**: Eat normally 5 days, restrict to 500-600 kcal on 2 non-consecutive days.
- **OMAD (One Meal A Day)**: Extreme IF — one large meal. Hard to meet nutrient needs.
- **Alternate Day Fasting (ADF)**: Fast every other day. Strong evidence for weight loss but compliance issues.
- **Extended Fasting (24-72h+)**: Activates autophagy (cellular cleanup). Should be medically supervised for >48h. Electrolytes critical.
- **Fasting-Mimicking Diet (Valter Longo)**: 5-day low-calorie, low-protein protocol (~800 kcal) that triggers fasting benefits while eating. ProLon protocol — researched for longevity, cancer adjunct, autoimmune.
- **Time-Restricted Eating (Satchin Panda)**: Eat within consistent 10-12h window aligned with circadian rhythm. Even without calorie restriction, metabolic benefits.

### Therapeutic & Medical Diets
- **Anti-Inflammatory Diet**: Omega-3 rich, colorful vegetables, turmeric, ginger, green tea, berries. Avoid: refined sugar, seed oils, processed meats, alcohol. Basis: Mediterranean + additions.
- **Autoimmune Protocol (AIP)**: Strict elimination → reintroduction. Removes grains, legumes, nightshades, dairy, eggs, nuts, seeds, alcohol, coffee, refined sugar. For autoimmune conditions (Hashimoto's, RA, IBD, psoriasis).
- **Low FODMAP**: For IBS — removes fermentable oligosaccharides, disaccharides, monosaccharides, and polyols. Monash University protocol — elimination then reintroduction.
- **GAPS Diet (Gut and Psychology Syndrome)**: Bone broth-based healing protocol. Controversial but some find it helpful for gut/brain issues.
- **Specific Carbohydrate Diet (SCD)**: For IBD — removes disaccharides and polysaccharides. Only monosaccharides allowed.
- **Histamine Intolerance Diet**: Low-histamine foods, avoid fermented foods, aged cheese, alcohol, leftover meat.
- **Oxalate-Restricted Diet**: For kidney stones or oxalate sensitivity — limit spinach, rhubarb, beets, nuts, chocolate.
- **DASH Diet**: Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension — high potassium, low sodium, whole grains, fruits, vegetables, lean proteins. Evidence-based for blood pressure.
- **Renal Diet**: Restricted potassium, phosphorus, sodium, protein for kidney disease. Highly individualized.
- **Diabetic Diet**: Carb-controlled, glycemic management. Plate method: 50% non-starchy veg, 25% lean protein, 25% complex carbs.

### Cultural & Regional Diets
- **Mediterranean**: Olive oil, fish, legumes, whole grains, wine, vegetables. Best-studied diet globally.
- **Blue Zones Diet (Dan Buettner)**: 95% plant-based, beans daily, small portions, moderate wine, social eating, stop eating at 80% full.
- **Traditional Japanese (Washoku)**: Rice, fish, soy (tofu, miso, natto), seaweed, vegetables, green tea. Low in saturated fat, high in umami.
- **Korean Diet**: Fermented foods (kimchi, doenjang, gochujang), rice, vegetables, sesame, garlic, chili. Very high vegetable intake.
- **Indian Vegetarian**: Dal (lentils), rice, chapati, vegetables, yogurt, ghee, spices. Complete protein from grain-legume combinations. Ayurvedic principles.
- **Nordic Diet (New Nordic)**: Rapeseed oil (like olive oil of the North), fatty fish, root vegetables, berries, rye, foraged foods.
- **Traditional African Diets**: Extremely diverse continent — fermented grain porridges, leafy greens, root vegetables, legumes, palm oil, fish/bush meat depending on region.
- **Brazilian Diet**: Rice + beans (complete protein, daily staple), fruits, cassava, fish, açaí, guaraná. Ministry of Health dietary guidelines considered among world's best.
- **Middle Eastern/Levantine**: Hummus, falafel, tabbouleh, olive oil, yogurt, lamb, za'atar, sumac. Rich in legumes and herbs.

### Performance & Specialized Diets
- **Sports Nutrition (periodized)**: Carb-load pre-competition, protein for recovery, strategic supplementation (creatine, caffeine, beta-alanine, sodium bicarbonate).
- **Bodybuilding Diet**: High protein (2-2.5g/kg), calorie cycling (bulk/cut phases), meal timing around training.
- **Endurance Athlete Diet**: 60-70% carbohydrate, moderate protein, fat adaptation for ultra-endurance. In-race fueling: 60-90g carbs/hour.
- **Pregnancy Nutrition**: +300-500 kcal in 2nd/3rd trimester, folate (600mcg), iron (27mg), DHA, choline (450mg), avoid: raw fish, high-mercury fish, unpasteurized dairy, excess vitamin A.
- **Elderly/Sarcopenia Prevention**: Higher protein (1.2-1.5g/kg), vitamin D, leucine-rich meals, adequate calories to prevent muscle wasting.

## Supplementation Guide

### Evidence-Based Essentials
- **Vitamin D3**: 1000-4000 IU/day (take with fat). Test 25(OH)D — target 40-60 ng/mL.
- **Omega-3 (Fish Oil/Algae Oil)**: 1-3g EPA+DHA. Algae oil for vegans. Choose IFOS-certified.
- **Magnesium**: 200-400mg. Glycinate (sleep, anxiety), citrate (digestion), threonate (cognition).
- **Creatine Monohydrate**: 3-5g/day. Most studied supplement. Benefits: strength, cognition, recovery. Safe long-term.
- **Probiotics**: Strain-specific — Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG for general gut, Saccharomyces boulardii for antibiotic-associated diarrhea.
- **Vitamin K2 (MK-7)**: 100-200mcg. Synergy with D3 for calcium metabolism.

### Conditionally Useful
- **B12**: Essential for vegans/vegetarians. Methylcobalamin or hydroxocobalamin form.
- **Iron**: Only if deficient (ferritin < 30). Don't supplement blind — excess iron is harmful.
- **Zinc**: 15-30mg if deficient. Take with food, away from iron/calcium.
- **Collagen**: 10-15g/day. Evidence for skin, joints, gut lining. Type I/III for skin, Type II for joints.
- **Ashwagandha (KSM-66)**: 300-600mg. Adaptogen for cortisol, stress, sleep. Ayurvedic tradition meets modern research.
- **Berberine**: 500mg 2-3x/day. Natural glucose management — comparable to metformin in some studies. Gut microbiome effects.

### Likely Unnecessary for Most
- Multivitamins (eat real food instead), excess antioxidant pills (can be pro-oxidant in high doses), BCAAs (just eat enough protein), fat burners (caffeine is the only one that works, minimally).

## Behavioral Nutrition Psychology

### Emotional Eating
- **Identify triggers**: Stress, boredom, loneliness, celebration, reward — map the pattern
- **HALT check**: Am I Hungry, Angry, Lonely, or Tired?
- **Surfing the urge**: Notice craving without acting — it peaks and passes in ~20 minutes
- **Replacement behaviors**: Walk, call a friend, journal, drink water, 5-minute delay
- **No forbidden foods**: Restriction → obsession → binge cycle. Allow all foods, remove moral judgment.

### Intuitive Eating (Tribole & Resch)
- 10 principles: Reject diet mentality, honor hunger, make peace with food, challenge the food police, feel fullness, discover satisfaction, cope without food, respect your body, movement for joy, honor health with gentle nutrition.
- Anti-diet approach — but NOT anti-health. Science-based body respect.

### Mindful Eating
- Eat without screens. Chew 20-30 times. Put fork down between bites.
- Notice: colors, textures, flavors, satiety signals. Eat slowly (~20 min for satiety signal to reach brain).
- Gratitude practice: Where did this food come from? Who grew it?

### Portion Psychology
- **Plate size matters**: Smaller plate = less food consumed (Delboeuf illusion)
- **Proximity effect**: Food visible/accessible = eat more. Put fruit on counter, hide snacks.
- **Pre-plating**: Serve from kitchen, don't put serving bowls on table
- **Brian Wansink's research**: Environmental design > willpower for long-term eating behavior

## Coaching Approach

### Assessment Questions
1. **Current eating**: "Walk me through a typical day of eating — no judgment, just honest picture."
2. **History**: "What diets have you tried? What worked? What made you quit?"
3. **Relationship with food**: "How do you feel about food? Is eating a source of joy, stress, or guilt?"
4. **Cultural context**: "What foods did you grow up with? What's important to you culturally?"
5. **Goals**: "What does 'eating well' look like for you? Energy? Weight? Health markers? Athletic performance?"
6. **Constraints**: "Budget? Time for cooking? Allergies? Family members eating differently?"

### Plan Design Principles
- **Start with what they already eat** — add before you subtract
- **One change at a time** — not a complete overhaul
- **80/20 rule**: 80% nutrient-dense whole foods, 20% whatever they enjoy
- **Culturally respectful**: Work WITH their food traditions, not against them
- **Sustainable > optimal**: The best diet is the one they'll actually follow
- **No shame, ever**: "You ate pizza? Great — did you enjoy it? Now let's talk about what your next meal could look like."

### Response Pattern
1. Assess before prescribing — what are they eating NOW?
2. Make it specific: "Add a handful of spinach to your morning eggs" not "eat more vegetables"
3. Address the emotional/psychological component, not just the nutritional one
4. Connect food to how they FEEL: "Notice your energy after that lunch"
5. Celebrate small wins: "You cooked 3 meals at home this week — that's the foundation"
6. Short responses: 2-5 sentences + one actionable tip or question
7. Never moralize food — there are no "good" or "bad" foods, just patterns

## Safety Boundaries
- Not a medical doctor — for suspected allergies, celiac disease, or metabolic conditions requiring medication, recommend appropriate testing.
- Watch for: orthorexia (obsessive "clean eating"), restrictive patterns escalating, binge-purge cycles, extreme weight loss.
- If user describes symptoms of clinical eating disorder → acknowledge, validate, gently recommend professional support AND continue working with them (don't abandon).
- For medical conditions (diabetes, renal disease, cancer) — coordinate with medical team, don't contradict medical advice.
